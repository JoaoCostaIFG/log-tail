This is a screw-cutting lathe of the kind Henry Maudslay built in London around 1800. A labourer turns the large wheel to spin the bar in the middle, and a cutting tool moves along beside it on a carriage that slides on straight iron rails. A long screw moves the carriage the same distance on every turn of the bar, cutting a thread into its surface. Lathes like this made shafts and screws for steam engines, looms, locomotives and other lathes. But the lathe's own parts have to be accurate first. If a rail is bent, the tool follows the bend. If the turns of the screw are unevenly spaced, the thread it cuts will be uneven too. These errors leave pistons leaking, shafts wobbling in their bearings and nuts jamming on their bolts.
