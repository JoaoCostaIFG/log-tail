noperts: Reproducible proofs of non-Rupert polyhedra

L 2 points · by fanf · github.com · discuss

This is a collection of formalized results about Noperts. A shape is said to be Rupert if it's possible to pass an exact copy of the shape through itself. It's named after Prince Rupert's Cube, the first famous demonstration that the cube can do this. Surprisingly, most convex polyhedra are Rupert, and it was not known whether any counterexamples even existed until 2025. We call a polyhedron that is not Rupert a "Nopert," for Nope + Rupert. You can learn more about Ruperts and Noperts in my 2025 SIGBOVIK paper Some upsetting things about shapes (that we already knew) or the later YouTube video Rupert's Snub Cube and other Math Holes , or if you want a more serious account, on Wikipedia . L 1 points · by typesanitizer · arxiv.org · discuss

Abstract: Data analysis applications typically aggregate data across many dimensions looking for anomalies or unusual patterns. The SQL aggregate functions and the GROUP BY operator produce zero-dimensional or one-dimensional aggregates. Applications need the N-dimensional generalization of these operators. This paper defines that operator, called the data cube or simply cube. The cube operator generalizes the histogram, cross-tabulation, roll-up, drill-down, and sub-total constructs found in most report writers. The novelty is that cubes are relations. Consequently, the cube operator can be imbedded in more complex non-procedural data analysis programs. The cube operator treats each of the N aggregation attributes as a dimension of N-space.
