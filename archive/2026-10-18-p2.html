c64-hw-sim simulates the motherboard of the Commodore 64, ASSY 250407, pin by pin. You can lift the leg of a chip, short it to ground or to +5V, pull a chip out of its socket, or open a resistor. Then you watch what the machine does: the picture on the monitor, the keyboard, every signal on the board. The program knows the board's netlist (the electrical connections on the PCB) and how each chip behaves at its pins. It knows nothing about what a broken Commodore 64 looks like: when you simulate a broken part and the screen fills with garbage, that garbage was computed from the circuit, not chosen by someone who knew what to expect.
