L 19 points · by abareplace · devblogs.microsoft.com · discuss

Some time ago, I mentioned that the ud2 instruction was created to be an architecturally undefined instruction . But why not just use an int 6 instruction? It’s a two-byte instruction, just like ud2 , and interrupt vector 6 is the invalid opcode vector, so a program could just invoke it manually and get whatever behavior the operating system assigns to an attempt to execute an invalid opcode. Architecturally, the invalid opcode is a fault , which means that the address of the faulting instruction is reported to the exception handler. On the other hand, the int 6 is a trap , which means that the address of the instruction after the int 6 is reported to the handler. The Annotated VT100 Firmware (2022)

L 14 points · by luke8086 · vt100.net · discuss

The VT100 terminal, launched in 1978, was DEC’s first ANSI-compatible terminal and their first that moved from discrete logic to a microprocessor, the Intel 8080. Slight modifications to the supported escape sequences were made for the cost-reduced follow-on models, the VT101 and VT102, and those changes became the definition of “VT100 compatible,” in DEC’s internal Video Systems Reference Manual , used for the next 15 years. This set of documents takes the original 8 KiB firmware for the VT100 and attempts to explain the workings.
