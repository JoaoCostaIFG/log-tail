Some time ago, I mentioned that the ud2 instruction was created to be an architecturally undefined instruction . But why not just use an int 6 instruction? It’s a two-byte instruction, just like ud2 , and interrupt vector 6 is the invalid opcode vector, so a program could just invoke it manually and get whatever behavior the operating system assigns to an attempt to execute an invalid opcode. Architecturally, the invalid opcode is a fault , which means that the address of the faulting instruction is reported to the exception handler. On the other hand, the int 6 is a trap , which means that the address of the instruction after the int 6 is reported to the handler.
