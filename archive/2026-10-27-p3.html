HN 138 points · by janandonly · archaeologymag.com · discuss

Archaeologists have found a huge network of Iron Age salt-mining chambers beneath Hallstatt , Austria. The chambers extend for at least 300 meters and offer new evidence about how prehistoric miners worked deep inside the mountain. People began living in the high valley above Hallstatt and producing salt about 7,300 years ago. Archaeologists have found Neolithic tools inside the mountain, including deer-antler picks and stone tools. By about 1200 BCE, salt production had reached a large scale, with workers producing around 1.5 tons of salt each day. Mining continued through several periods, even after major landslides filled earlier underground workings. The landslides buried the empty chambers with material from the surface.
