Elektronika BK-0010 ran on a processor copied from a minicomputer, not a microcomputer. Where the West built home computers around 8-bit chips designed for the job, the Soviet Union’s only officially sanctioned home computer used the K1801VM1, a 16-bit clone of the CPU inside DEC’s PDP-11, repurposed for a machine that cost four months of an average wage. That PDP-11 heritage is the whole story. The K1801VM1 gave the BK-0010 real 16-bit instructions and addressing at a time when Z80 and 6502 machines were the Western norm, and it made the computer genuinely capable once you got past the 16 KiB of RAM left over after the video buffer took its half.
